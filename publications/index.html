Securing Autonomous Chemical Robots Through Physical and Digital Containment

Autonomous chemical robots have the potential to revolutionise molecular discovery, yet their power also exposes chemistry to new forms of risk. Without intrinsic containment, these systems could execute reactions beyond human oversight, posing hazards both within the laboratory and across the digital landscape. Here we present the Control of Substances Hazardous to Health chemputer (CoSHHputer), a secure autonomous synthesis platform that integrates physical protection, digital encryption, and virtual replication to perform and verify hazardous reactions safely at gram scale

Achieving Operational Universality through a Turing-Complete Chemputer

The Turing Machine is the fundamental abstraction behind all modern computers, and any modern computer can simulate one, an equivalence known as Turing completeness. A system with this property can, in theory, perform any algorithmically defined task through discrete unit operations. Here, we extend this concept to robotic platforms that execute physical operations for chemical synthesis via a chemically-aware programming language, XDL

Programmable Microwaveable Chemistry in the Chemputer

Switched “On” Transient Fluorescence Output from a Pulsed-Fuel Molecular Ratchet

Molecular Machines Multi-step Synthesis

Switched “On” Transient Fluorescence Output from a Pulsed-Fuel Molecular Ratchet

We report the synthesis and operation of a molecular energy ratchet that transports a crown ether from solution onto a thread, along the axle, over a fluorophore, and off the other end of the thread back into bulk solution, all in response to a single pulse of a chemical fuel (CCl3CO2H). The system provides a potential alternative signaling approach for artificial molecular machines that read symbols from sequence-encoded molecular tapes.

J. Am. Chem. Soc. 145, 49 (2023)

Applications of Fuelled Artificial Molecular Machines

Nature has evolved highly efficient biological machines that use chemical fuels to drive directional molecular motion and complex reactions with exceptional precision. Mimicking this control at the molecular level could revolutionise fields such as materials science, surface chemistry, and drug delivery. BCF-Catalysed Coupling of N-alkyl Arylamines & Alkenes for the Synthesis of Tetrahydroquinolines

Catalysis

BCF-Catalysed Coupling of N-alkyl Arylamines & Alkenes for the Synthesis of Tetrahydroquinolines

Tetrahydroquinolines feature in molecules with wide ranging properties, including those with biological activity and materials applications. This report demonstrates that widely accessible N-alkyl arylamines can be directly coupled with new classes of alkenes to synthesise tetrahydroquinolines. The reaction is facilitated by a commercially available electron-deficient borane catalyst, and enables the creation of novel tetrahydroquinoline scaffolds.

Org. Lett. 27 (2025)
